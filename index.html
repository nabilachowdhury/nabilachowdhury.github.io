Nabila Chowdhury

Doctoral Candidate · Faculty of Information, University of Toronto

I am a PhD candidate at the Faculty of Information, University of Toronto, supervised by Dr. Negin Dahya and Dr. Cosmin Munteanu. My research sits at the intersection of human–computer interaction, memory, and migration. I study how immersive and emerging technologies — VR, AR, and AI — can support people in authoring personal narratives, preserving culture, and sustaining connection across generations.

I am currently on academic leave, working as a Research Analyst — UX and Conversational AI at the Faculty of Engineering, Systems Design Engineering, University of Waterloo.

Previously I completed my MSc in Computer Science at the University of Manitoba, where I worked in the HCI Lab with Dr. Celine Latulipe and Dr. James E. Young, studying intergenerational music co-listening between grandparents and teenage grandchildren.

Projects

Authoring Memory Narratives in VR

PhD project · 2023 – present

How can virtual reality support people in curating and sharing personal memory, particularly in the context of migration? I run memory-authoring workshops with migrant participants inside immersive environments and study how people compose, revisit, and narrate their own histories in space.

Demo & code Paper (CSCW 2025)

Culture, Memory, Migration & Technology

With Dr. Negin Dahya, Dr. Cosmin Munteanu, Dr. Ishtiaque Ahmed · 2022 – 2025

A multi-community study of how grandparents and grandchildren preserve and exchange memory and culture across migration contexts, and what role technology plays in intergenerational memory curation. My work spans focus groups, interviews, and thematic analysis across several diaspora communities.

Paper (CHI 2024)

Prompt-Based Bias in AI-Generated Art

With Dr. Anastasia Kuzminykh, in collaboration with Naver · 2023

Beyond the biases baked into a model, how do a user's own prompts surface biased output? This project examines prompt-triggered bias in AI art generation tools, with the aim of helping people write prompts that produce fairer results.

Paper (GenAICHI 2023)

Older Adults & Conversational Agents

With Dr. Anastasia Kuzminykh · 2022

An investigation of how older adults perceive the voices of smart speakers such as Alexa and Google Home — which voice characteristics they prefer, and why — in order to design conversational agents that actually suit them.

Intergenerational Music Co-Listening

MSc thesis, with Dr. Celine Latulipe & Dr. James E. Young · 2020 – 2022

An exploratory study of how grandparents and teenage grandchildren use technology to be online together and talk about music they love, and of the social and technical barriers that make those moments hard to sustain.

Poster (CSCW 2021) Journal article (2022)

StoryTime: Collaborative Story Creation

UIST Student Innovation Competition · 2021

A tangible interface built on toio robots that lets a grandparent and grandchild take turns building a story together and recording audio narration, supporting remote, playful intergenerational interaction. Selected as one of twelve teams.

Video Paper

Shonabondhu

Undergraduate capstone, with Dr. Nova Ahmed · North South University

A flash-flood warning system for rural Bangladesh, combining IoT sensing with HCI-led system design to give communities advance notice before flooding reaches them.
